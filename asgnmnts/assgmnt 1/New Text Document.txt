This can also help us to consider possible shared and divergent philosophical (ontological, epistemological, etc.) orientations within overarching typologies. For example, when Alvesson and Sk ö ldberg include post-positivism with positivism, they focus on conceptions of reality and, to a lesser extent, their epistemological and methodological implications (suggesting the first is more, though not wholly, orientated to quantitative data, the second to qualitative, and the third to mixed methods). On the other hand, Saunders et al. (2016) present a model in relation to all four -ologies (methodology more focused around likely methods). While not including post-positivism, they expand the typology and present interpretivism as an apparent synonym for Alvesson and Sk ö ldberg’s (2009) constructivism (with social construction included as an ontological factor). Variations in the jargon of the philosophy of science inevitably make its study more complex, but it is important that, as researchers, we consider its implications for our own research.
